As COVID-19 vaccines are being rolled out across the globe, many have wondered whether pregnant and lactating people can or should be vaccinated as part of broader immunization efforts. Countries around the world have taken a variety of positions - ranging from highly restrictive policies that bar access to vaccines based on pregnancy or lactation status to widely permissive positions in which all pregnant or lactating people can receive vaccines, and in some cases, are recommended and encouraged to do so. 


The variability in policy positions is in part a consequence of the absence of evidence on vaccine products in pregnancy and lactation, with pregnant and lactating women excluded from the vast majority of trials conducted prior to authorization. Efforts are underway to improve the evidence base, including through observational studies of pregnant and lactating people who receive vaccines as well as some randomized controlled trials. In the meantime, however, public health authorities and recommending bodies are developing guidance on vaccines and pregnancy with far less evidence than they have for most other populations. 


Policy positions on vaccination in pregnancy or lactation are important everywhere; on any given day over 150 million people in the world are pregnant and <span class="bg-warning">XXX</span> are lactating. Pregnant women are at elevated risk of severe COVID-19 disease and death, and of preterm labor. 


Vaccine policy in pregnancy is particularly urgent and important in countries with very constrained vaccine supply. Health workers, a top priority group for this context, are majority female and many are pregnant or breastfeeding. Many other priority groups with underlying medical conditions or in high exposure occupations or settings may also be pregnant or lactating. Additionally, pregnancy has been shown to increase the risk of severe COVID-19 disease and contribute to higher rates of preterm birth. 


The COVID-19 Maternal Immunization Tracker (COMIT) documents where various countries fall on the spectrum. It provides a global snapshot of public health policies that shape access to COVID-19 vaccines for pregnant and lactating people. The default display presents the most permissive position across all vaccines in a given country to convey at a glance whether  pregnant or lactating individuals are allowed or encouraged to receive any vaccine currently authorized for use in that setting. COMIT also allows users to explore the data by vaccine product to see if there are differences in pregnancy policies between products, both within and between countries, as well as by recommending body to see how recommendations of public health agencies differ from those of professional medical societies. Additional details are provided in tables on a country-by-country basis below the maps. Because this is a dynamic space, with countries and other recommending bodies continuing to update their positions based on the changing epidemiological situation, emerging evidence on vaccine products, and growing understanding of risks of COVID-19 disease in pregnancy, COMIT is updated on a regular basis to reflect changes in policy positions.
